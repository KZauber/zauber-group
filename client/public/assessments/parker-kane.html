Your blog has published 32 posts since last September, all aimed at the town searches; six carry "luxury" in the URL, and none of them ranks for it.

The encouraging half: the town pages prove the domain is trusted. The fix is positioning, not rebuilding — retitle and rewrite the pages you already have for the buyer who searches with "luxury," and add the one page you're missing for San Antonio and the Hill Country.
03 Website Structure
B
The site is better built than most in this market. You have a real project portfolio with eight completed homes, a documented build process, and — the part that matters most — dedicated city pages for Boerne, Bulverde and Fair Oaks Ranch. Most builders we assess have none. Those pages are the reason you win the town-level search.

The basics are handled, too. Eleven of your twelve pages carry a written meta description — the line Google shows under your name in results — and they read like a person wrote them. The one exception is Our Process, which has none, so Google picks its own snippet for it. Your San Antonio page sits at pkcustomhomes.com/san-antonio-2/; the "-2" is a leftover from an earlier version of the page, but the old address now redirects cleanly to this one and only one San Antonio page is live. That's cosmetic, not a ranking problem.

Where the site does leak: pkcustomhomes.com/author/parkerkanehome/ — an automatically generated WordPress author archive — is open to search engines and listed in your sitemap. It's a thin listing page with your business name on it and no description, competing for attention with your real pages. It should be closed off from search.
04 Social Media
C
Facebook is your strongest social asset: 1,294 page likes on the platform where the 40-to-65-year-old move-up buyer in your service area actually spends their time. Instagram, YouTube and Houzz are all linked from the site as well. For a family-run builder, that's a respectable spread.

What's missing is the job those channels should be doing. 1,294 Facebook likes and no visible review funnel pointed at them. That audience already likes you — many of them are past clients, subs, and neighbors of homes you've built. It is the single cheapest source of the Google reviews you're short on, and nothing currently asks them.

Custom home content also performs unusually well organically — walkthroughs, framing-to-finish sequences, Hill Country lot tours. A builder with 19 years of completed projects has more raw material here than almost any other kind of local business.
05 Reviews & Reputation
C
4.7 stars across 11 Google reviews, plus 5 out of 5 on Houzz. The rating is genuinely good. The volume is the problem: eleven reviews for a builder founded in 2007 means the overwhelming majority of your happy clients were never asked.

At eleven reviews, every single review carries about nine percent of your public reputation. One detailed critical review — there's one raising schedule overruns, cost, and an unresolved garage door issue — sits permanently near the top of a pool that small. That same review inside a pool of sixty would be a rounding error a prospect scrolls past. The fastest way to neutralize a bad review is never to fight it; it's to bury it in volume.

Review count is also a direct ranking signal for Google Maps and one of the strongest inputs to which builders AI tools recommend. At eleven, you're competing for those recommendations with one hand tied. Fixing this is the highest-return, lowest-effort item on the entire list — the clients already exist.
06 Paid Advertising
Needs Live Check
We found no evidence of active paid campaigns — but we want to be straight with you: absence of evidence from the outside isn't proof, and we're not going to grade you on a guess. Confirming this takes five minutes inside your Meta Business and Google Ads accounts.

One thing we can see from outside: the only tracking on your site is Google Analytics. There's no Meta Pixel and no Google Ads conversion tag. If campaigns are running, nothing on the site is measuring what they bring in — and if they aren't, that's the first thing to install before spending a dollar.

What we can say is that this channel is the one with the most leverage in your specific situation. You have professional project photography and a defined geography — Boerne, Bulverde, Fair Oaks Ranch, Castroville, Pipe Creek, Canyon Lake, Mico. At custom-build contract values, one signed client returns the cost of a year of advertising many times over. No other channel in this market has that math.
07 Lead Follow-Up
Needs Live Check
Your contact page publishes a real phone number — (210) 771-5517 — and a real email, hello@pkcustomhomes.com. That's more than a lot of builders do, and it matters: buyers at this price point want to reach a person.

That window is where custom-home leads are won and lost — a buyer researching builders is contacting three or four the same afternoon, and the one who responds first with substance sets the terms of the comparison.
